Lab EM waves · §1.3 Polarisation · equation (3)

Polarisation from γ and φ

E(z) = |E| ( cos γ 1x + sin γ ejφ 1y ) e−jkz

Two perpendicular waves with the same frequency, added together. γ decides how the amplitude is shared between the x-wave and the y-wave. φ shifts the y-wave in time relative to the x-wave. The tip of their sum traces a line, a circle or an ellipse: that shape is the polarisation.

Conventions as in the lab text: phasors use ejωt, so E(z,t) = Re{E(z) ejωt} with |E| = 1. Handedness follows the IEEE rule: point your right thumb along +z; if your fingers curl the way the tip turns, the wave is right-handed. That makes γ = π/4, φ = −π/2 right-hand circular, as in figure 4b. In the end-on view +z points at you, so right-handed tips turn counterclockwise there.
